An <Term id="ocho" /> is a step followed by a pivot, repeated. The eight it draws on the floor
is a by-product, not a goal — chase the shape and you will get a wobble; do the step and the
pivot properly and the shape appears on its own.

## Forward and back

**Back ochos** are usually taught first: the follower steps back, pivots, steps back the other
way. **Forward ochos** travel forwards around the leader and ask for more from the standing leg.

## Keeping the follower on the line

A useful exercise from the leader's side: keep the follower travelling along a straight line
while they pivot. To do that, the leader has to move in a zig-zag — out to one side, back
across, out to the other — while the follower's line stays straight.

<Callout type="question">
Could you make it completely circular instead? Yes — turn the follower's first step into a
forward ocho and the third into a back ocho, and what you have is a turn. The last steps are
simply the exit.
</Callout>
