<Term id="disassociation" /> is turning the chest away from the hips while both stay level.
The pivot is what happens when you let that stored twist unwind through the standing foot.

## Two things that are not the same

**Rotation** is the whole rectangle turning together — shoulders and hips as one piece.
**Twist** is the shoulders going one way while the hips hold. Rotation plus travel gives you a
cross. Twist gives you ochos and turns. Most people who "cannot pivot" are actually rotating.

<Accordion title="Where the pivot happens" tool="gears" label="Technique">

- On the ball of the standing foot, not the heel and not the whole flat foot.
- With the free leg collected or extended along the floor — never held in the air, which
  costs you the balance you need.
- Level hips. If one hip lifts, the twist has leaked upwards.
- The standing leg stays long. Sinking mid-pivot is what makes a pivot feel stuck.

</Accordion>

## Take it to the practica

<Exercise
  id="pivots-basics"
  title="Pivots"
  intro="Do these barefoot or in socks on a smooth floor before you do them in heels."
  items={[
    'Stand on one foot, collected. Turn the chest as far as it goes without the hips following. Hold.',
    { text: 'Same again, then let the hips catch up and see how far you travelled.', note: 'That distance is your pivot.' },
    'Pivot 90 degrees, stop, check balance, pivot back.',
    { text: 'Pivot in both directions on both feet.', note: 'One of the four will be much worse. Spend your time there.' },
    'Add the pivot to a back step, then to a forward step.',
  ]}
