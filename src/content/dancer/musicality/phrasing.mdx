Beats come in groups. In tango, almost always groups of eight, and those groups come in fours.
Hearing the group is what lets you stop *deliberately* instead of stopping because you ran out
of ideas.

## Counting in eights

Put on a tango and count 1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8, over and over. You will feel the music arrive
somewhere at the end of each eight — a resolution, a breath, a slight settling. That is a
phrase.

Four of those phrases usually make a section, and the end of a section is unmistakable once
you have noticed it: the music lands harder, and often something changes right afterwards —
the singer enters, the arrangement thins out, the orchestra restates the melody.

<Accordion title="What to do with a phrase ending" tool="sliders" label="Ideas" open>

- **Pause.** The most powerful and least used option.
- **Arrive somewhere.** Finish a cross, complete a turn, land the follower on their axis.
- **Change something.** Switch from single to double time, from linear to circular, from
  travelling to staying.
- **Change nothing, on purpose.** Walking straight through a phrase ending is an option too,
  as long as it was a choice.

</Accordion>

<Callout type="tip" title="The cheapest musicality there is">
Stop at the end of the phrase. That is it. A dancer who pauses in the right place looks
musical even if everything in between was a plain walk — and a dancer who never pauses looks
busy no matter how good the steps were.
</Callout>

<KeyPoints title="Remember">

- Eight beats to a phrase, four phrases to a section.
- The end of the phrase is where the choices live.
- Being early or late to a phrase ending is much more noticeable than being early or late to a beat.

</KeyPoints>

## Take it to the practica

<Exercise
  id="phrasing-listening"
  title="Hearing phrases"
  intro="Sitting down first. It is much easier to hear when you are not also trying to dance."
  items={[
    'Count eights through a whole D\'Arienzo track without losing your place.',
    { text: 'Raise a hand at the end of every phrase.', note: 'Then check: did you raise it at 8, or at 1 of the next one?' },
    'Do it again with a Di Sarli, where the endings are softer.',
    'Find the moment the singer enters. Count backwards — it is almost always the start of a section.',
  ]}
/>

    { text: 'Dance a whole tanda where the only rule is "something happens at 8".', note: 'It does not matter what.' },
