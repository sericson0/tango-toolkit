Tango has a strong, regular pulse, and for a long time your only job is to be on it. That
sounds like a low bar. It is not — most dancers who think they are on the beat are arriving
just after it, because they start moving *on* the beat rather than arriving on it.

<KeyPoints title="The whole page in three lines">

- Arrive on the beat. Leave before it.
- The strong beat is 1 and 3; the interesting one is often 2 and 4.
- If you cannot hear it, change the orchestra before you change yourself.

</KeyPoints>

## Hearing it

Tango is counted in four. The **1** and the **3** are the strong beats — the ones a marching
band would put a bass drum on. In most Golden Age tango, something percussive is landing
right there: a piano left hand, a bandoneón chord, the bass.

<Accordion title="If you genuinely cannot find the beat" tool="sliders" label="Troubleshooting" open>

Start with an orchestra that makes it obvious. **Juan D'Arienzo** is the standard answer, and
it is the standard answer for a reason — the beat is not hiding anywhere in a 1930s D'Arienzo.

Then work outwards:

- **Easy:** D'Arienzo, Biagi, Tanturi, Rodríguez
- **Medium:** Di Sarli, Troilo, D'Agostino, Caló
- **Harder:** Pugliese, Fresedo, late Troilo, anything from the 1950s onwards

If you are learning on Pugliese, you are not bad at hearing the beat. You have picked the
hardest orchestra in the room.

</Accordion>

## Arriving, not departing

Here is the thing that changes people's dancing: **the beat is when your weight lands**, not
when you start to move. If you begin the step on the beat, you land somewhere after it, and
the whole dance sits slightly behind the music.

<Callout type="tip" title="Test it against a partner">
Walk with someone and both count out loud. Now agree that the count happens at the moment the
foot takes weight. Almost everyone has to start moving noticeably earlier than feels natural.
</Callout>

<Callout type="remark">
This is also why a slow, deliberate walk is harder than a quick one. The slower the step, the
longer you have to plan the arrival — and the more obvious it is when you did not.
</Callout>

## Single time, double time, and half time

Once you can arrive reliably, you have three speeds available without changing anything else:

- **Single time** — one step per beat. The default.
- **Double time** — two steps per beat. Not "hurried"; the same distance, subdivided.
- **Half time** — one step every two beats. This is where a walk starts to feel enormous.

The skill is not doing any one of them. It is changing between them because the music changed,
and being back on the pulse at the end of it.
